## 10. Runtime cost and coupling analysis
- The runner performs five fixed WordPress snapshot bootstraps plus two additional bootstraps per executed hook: 5 + 2N, yielding 5/7/9/11 bootstraps for zero/one/two/three executions.
- Deep snapshot computation runs 5 + N times per cycle. Each computes owned/global cron data, Action Scheduler state, claim counts, real-due queries, and protected business fingerprints.
- Protected snapshots scan multiple post types and then call get_post_meta per record/key, creating data-volume-dependent query and memory cost. They are currently computed even though the runner stability assertion does not compare the business fingerprint.
- Full and non-allowlisted cron serialization are modest at the current 34-event scale but couple owned execution availability to unrelated scheduler drift.
- Action Scheduler scans and hard-coded ID32733 checks couple this runner to a separate recovery state that cannot remain fixed forever.
- Communications parity performs two full filesystem tree hash traversals every 15 minutes.
- Guard registration and counter bookkeeping are cheap relative to WordPress bootstraps, tree hashing and DB-wide protected snapshots.
- Recorded evidence, without inventing benchmarks: check-only with five bootstraps took 9 seconds; natural cycles with one/two/three executions took 14/18/19 seconds. The fixed deep preflight cost is material but current safety controls explain why activation was reliable.
